# Life Update: Working at Bevel!

I met Louis, the CEO and co-founder of [Bevel](https://www.trybevel.ai/), on LinkedIn back in the spring. We commented on the same post, started talking in the replies, and then he asked if I wanted to hop on a call. For the record: this is the only time in my life I've agreed to get on a call with a LinkedIn stranger. I've had plenty of LinkedIn conversations before, sure, but none that actually led anywhere. Amazingly, this one did! Maybe LinkedIn CAN get you jobs (let's not talk about the probably 40+ other jobs I applied to that seemed to merely dissipate into the hellscape of that job-searching netherworld.)

Anyhow, Louis was building a tool to sync a well-trained AI with an LMS, one that could scan a course and catch in seconds what used to take a team of people months. I walked away so impressed that I immediately raved about it to my colleagues, mostly because I'd been trying to duct-tape together some version of this myself, just scoped down to my own tiny corner of the work. Louis and his co-founder, Brian, were solving a problem I'd been living inside of for over a decade, so I immediately saw the potential.

What was that problem, you ask? If you've never done instructional design, you should know that a huge amount of the job is what I call "in the weeds" work. Work like checking for typos, confirming a learning outcome actually aligns with the assessment question meant to measure it, and writing a new question because it doesn't. At Lumen, we'd sit on continuous improvement work like this for months (sometimes even longer!) not because we didn't care, but because crawling through a course to find one small misalignment, then fixing it in the four different places it showed up, was such a slog that it kept losing to whatever was more urgent at the time.

I'd guess instructional designers could easily spend something like 30% of their time on that kind of work, obviously with a ton of variation. Bevel can chew through a lot of it in seconds. That doesn't put instructional designers out of a job; it means that the 30% might shrink down to 10%, and the hours that used to vanish into the weeds go somewhere better, like rethinking how a whole course is structured, building something more ambitious than a skills map, or thinking more about who's on the other end of this course and what they need from it.

That's what I like about what Bevel is trying to do...not replace instructional designers, but hand them back the part of the job that made us want to do this in the first place. Education is changing fast right now, faster than most of us can keep a clean read on, and nobody quite knows what it looks like once things settle. But I think that some things aren't going anywhere. We'll still need skills mapping, clear learning outcomes, some honest accounting of what's actually inside a course. Bevel's bet is that AI can handle the tedious middle of that work, so people get more time for the thinking part.

So, a few months after Lumen let me go, and a few months of freelancing my way into this, I'm now Head of Product & Customer Success at Bevel. It sounds quite different, but if you squint, a lot of it's the same because I'm still figuring out what's broken or not working, figuring out how to fix it, and teaching people along the way. These days I'm spending a lot of my time vibe-coding my way through projects, like an early version of a customer dashboard, and a prompt library to help our customers get more out of the tool. I've been in a lot of calls, and have enjoyed seeing them experience that same type of awe when they first see what Bevel can do. I'm wearing multiple hats, drinking out of a firehose of "Startup 101," and enjoying all of the new challenges. I'm excited for the opportunity and looking forward to seeing where this new adventure takes me!
