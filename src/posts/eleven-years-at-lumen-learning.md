# 11+ Years at Lumen Learning

<img src="{{ '/images/LumenTeam.png' | url }}" alt="The Lumen Learning team together" class="post-featured-image" />

This month, I was let go from Lumen Learning as part of a reduction in force. It's bittersweet. I had a little bit of a heads up that it was coming, so it wasn't the worst kind of layoff, but it was still sad. I've spent the last few days sitting with that sadness but also feeling a whole lot of gratitude for the nearly twelve years that came before it.

I started with Lumen in the fall of 2014, as a contractor, on the recommendation of David Wiley. I'd just left a teaching job, moved to Colorado, and was figuring out how to parent two babies (spoiler: I'd add another and am still figuring this out!). I'd met David years earlier through Mountain Heights Academy (formerly the Open High School of Utah), where he sat on the founding board and I got the opportunity to teach his son. It was there where I first understood what open educational resources could really do, and where I went to my first Open Education Conference. I knew that anything that David was building was worth being a part of, so when he mentioned this new startup called Lumen Learning needed some help, I of course said yes right away.

The early work was odd jobs and courseware built to the specs of a grant we'd received, but that shifted pretty quickly into something with more autonomy: designing courses for Candela, our first platform. In the summer of 2015, my family took a trip out to Oregon and Washington, and I finally met Kim and the rest of the team in person. I remember being struck by how much talent and heart was packed into that small group…and really that feeling never wore off over the decade that followed. I got to know Joni, Nate, Tom, Shelley, Kelvin, Suzanne, Bracken, Alexis, and Vallerie in those early years. I even stayed with Alexis for my first All Hands meeting! I've since grown close to so many fellow Learning Team members over the years and am thankful for their friendships. Fortunately, I still live only 10 minutes away from Wen, so we still get to hang out often, which is pretty incredible for a company that pulled talent from across the country.

<img src="{{ '/images/Axethrowing.JPG' | url }}" alt="Team axe throwing at a Lumen Learning offsite" class="post-inline-image" />

All of our All Hands meetings and holiday parties were memorable, but one of my favorite memories was a multi-day meeting at McMenamins and another was a trip to the Oregon coast, where we finally sat down and hashed out job titles for the Learning Team. We landed on Course Product Manager, and it fit nicely because we were doing real product and project management, but we were also instructional designers, curating OER and building every piece of the courseware ourselves. As the team grew, our gatherings became something more standard, with one or two visits a year to the Portland offices. I'll miss those. I was always stuck on East Coast time when I was out there, so I'd wake up absurdly early and go running through the Rose Garden or along the Willamette River before anyone else was up.

Ross Strader was my manager for the longest stretch, and he led our little team well. We had good 1:1s, ridiculous trivia during meetings, and a rapport I looked forward to every week. Ross is a big part of why I gained real confidence as an instructional designer. He brought me along to present with him at a conference in Vancouver, back when we were deep into figuring out how to make OER interactive, and we ran a workshop together on building things in H5P.

<img src="{{ '/images/LumenLunch.JPG' | url }}" alt="Team lunch with Lumen Learning colleagues" class="post-inline-image" />

It was around this time we moved from Candela to Waymaker, and we saw a big improvement in our course quality, with a huge emphasis on targeted feedback, continuous improvements, and interactivity. The Intro Psych course I'd been building started doing really well, and I got to build out the rest of our Psychology catalog from there. I built some great working relationships with subject matter experts over those years—Steve Greenlaw at the University of Mary Washington, Pat Carroll at UT Austin, Jessica Traylor at Gordon State, Mark Lempke, an all-around historical all-star, and so many others. I loved working with SMEs, training them on our platform and processes, learning from their expertise, and building something meaningful together.

<img src="{{ '/images/Lumenbooth.png' | url }}" alt="Lumen Learning conference booth" class="post-inline-image" />

Ross eventually had to leave us, but our Learning Team's success continued under Giselle as we transitioned from Waymaker to Lumen One. That shift coincided with me finishing my master's program in instructional design, and I was eager to dig into the courses and do even better needs analysis to see where they really needed work. We honed in on our goals as a company and as course-builders: to enable unprecedented learning for all students, and to eliminate race and income as predictors of success in college courses. That focus meant there were new opportunities for more customized content that was made more relatable to students. My favorite part was that it also meant better support for instructors through detailed lesson plans, activities, and tools to help them succeed. When I got to teach at Davidson College using our new Lumen One Psych course, I recognized all of the ways our courseware needed to better support faculty and students, and it was so rewarding to be able to go back into the course and make these essential changes.

After nearly twelve years, I'm proud of what I was able to build at Lumen. I'm grateful for the people I got to work with, for how much I grew professionally, for the confidence I built in my own abilities along the way. I'm grateful, too, for two sabbaticals with my family that I will never take for granted (hello, Peru and Europe!). It's hard to believe that chapter is closing, but I'll cherish it forever. I only wish I had taken more pictures along the way!

I'm already doing some freelance work with a company I'm excited about, Bevel (more on that soon). And I'm using this stretch of unplanned time to take stock of my own skills, dig further into Claude Code and what it might mean for instructional designers, and follow a few threads I've been curious about for a while.

Stay tuned for what's next.
